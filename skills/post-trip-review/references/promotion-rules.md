# Learning promotion rules

Promote only when the lesson is expected to matter across future trips. A trip learning is cheap to get wrong. A profile sentence is not.

Worked examples:

- "That Midtown hotel was convenient for this conference." Trip `post_trip_learnings`. It is about one property on one trip.
- "For work trips I always want to be within walking distance of the venue." Profile `work_travel`. The traveler stated the general rule themselves.
- "The 6 am departure ruined the first day; I never want flights that early again." Likely profile `flight_preferences`. Confirm first if it reads as a one-off complaint rather than a general rule.
- "I loved this one restaurant." Trip learning, or possibly profile `past_trips`. Not a `food_dining` rule unless the traveler identifies the broader cuisine or dining preference behind it.
- "I realized I need a slower itinerary with one major activity a day." Profile `pace_planning`, if the traveler confirms it is general.

Never promote a vendor-specific success or failure into a permanent category preference. One bad rental car counter is not a preference about rental cars.

When a learning is conditional, keep the condition. "Walking distance to the venue on work trips" is more useful than "close to the center", and flattening it into an absolute rule makes it wrong on leisure trips.

Before writing, read the target profile section and carry its existing sentences forward. A promoted lesson that replaces the section it lands in has cost the traveler more than it gained them.
